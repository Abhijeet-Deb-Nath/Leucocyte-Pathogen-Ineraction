Yes — your setup needs to change.

Right now the simulator is structurally biased toward “distributed evasive population beats one traveler,” not toward rich immune–pathogen interaction. The main reason is that the current environment is a large open arena with a long horizon, one macrophage, global macrophage vision, bacterial replication anywhere, and bacteria winning by survival-to-timeout or population cap. In your code, that comes from `GRID_SIZE = 30`, `TIME_HORIZON = 300`, `INITIAL_BACTERIA_COUNT = 5`, `BACTERIA_REPLICATION_PROB = 0.02`, macrophage full-board visibility, and bacteria also choosing actions from effectively global state, while the macrophage’s MCTS is actually off because `ROLLOUT_BUDGET = 0`.   

So your diagnosis is right: the problem is no longer “can the macrophage outthink the bacteria?” It has become “can one unit traverse enough space fast enough to suppress a reproducing dispersed process?” That is why dumb cornering feels nearly unbeatable.

The best fix is **not** just tuning counts. You need to redesign the environment so that it creates **forced local interaction, meaningful trade-offs, and state switching costs**.

## What is unrealistic in the current setup

There are five major realism failures.

### 1) The map is an empty Euclidean board

Real lung or tissue infection is not a clean 30×30 empty chessboard. It is patchy, compartmentalized, and locally structured. Acute pulmonary *P. aeruginosa* infection is shaped by local deposition, local immune sensing, and local neutrophil recruitment rather than free global roaming. Resident alveolar macrophages help initiate neutrophil recruitment, and neutrophils are the dominant acute bacterial clearers in the lung. ([PMC][1])

### 2) Bacteria can replicate almost anywhere

In your simulator, any non-adjacent bacterium can attempt replication every step with base probability, and nutrients only boost it further. That makes space itself too valuable. 

### 3) There is no recruitment layer

Biologically, macrophages are not the whole first-line story in alveolar infection. They sense and recruit; neutrophils then arrive in force and do much of the acute killing. ([PMC][1])

### 4) Biofilm is modeled as a free local buff

Real *P. aeruginosa* does not always want biofilm. Biofilm trades motility and exploration for protection and persistence; c-di-GMP is a central switch in that trade-off. High c-di-GMP promotes attachment/EPS and suppresses motility, while lower c-di-GMP favors dispersal and motility. ([PMC][2])

### 5) There is no real cost-of-infection objective

The host currently mainly cares about kill-or-timeout. But in biology, a costly immune win and a clean immune win are very different. In acute and chronic *Pseudomonas* infection, excessive neutrophilic inflammation can damage tissue even when it is part of clearance. ([PMC][1])

## The core redesign principle

You want the game to stop rewarding “maximize pairwise distance forever” and start rewarding things like:

* when to attach versus disperse
* when to hide versus replicate
* when to recruit help versus conserve damage
* when to commit to killing a local colony versus patrol elsewhere
* when bacterial clustering is beneficial and when it becomes dangerous

That means you need new knobs in three layers:

### Layer A: geometry and contact

These knobs decide whether agents are forced into meaningful encounters.

### Layer B: ecology and regulation

These knobs decide whether bacterial states have trade-offs.

### Layer C: immune system staging

These knobs decide whether macrophage decisions have consequences beyond its own body.

## The knobs I would tune first

### 1) Replace global open space with compartmentalized local niches

Add **alveolar pockets / airway patches / epithelial surfaces** instead of one open board.

New knobs:

* `N_COMPARTMENTS`
* `COMPARTMENT_SIZE`
* `AIRWAY_CONNECTIVITY`
* `SURFACE_PATCHES`
* `MUCUS_PATCHES`
* `PASSAGE_BOTTLENECK_WIDTH`

Why this helps:
A bacterium in one compartment should not be able to force a macrophage to traverse the whole world every time. Instead, interaction becomes local. This is much closer to the biology of alveolar hotspots and local recruitment. ([PMC][3])

Good effect on algorithms:
Now planning is about **which compartment to prioritize**, not just shortest chase over a blank grid.

---

### 2) Make sensing local and noisy, not omniscient

Right now your bacteria’s decision tree uses macrophage position and nutrient positions directly, and the macrophage has full-board vision in config.  

New knobs:

* `BACTERIA_SENSE_RADIUS_MACROPHAGE`
* `BACTERIA_SENSE_RADIUS_NUTRIENT`
* `MACROPHAGE_SENSE_RADIUS`
* `CHEMOKINE_GRADIENT_NOISE`
* `LINE_OF_SIGHT_BLOCKS`

Why this helps:
It stops the benchmark from collapsing into geometric perfect-information evasion. It creates richer planning under uncertainty.

Good effect on algorithms:
Now search and policy quality matter more, because agents must infer from partial cues.

---

### 3) Make replication conditional on local colonization, not free anywhere

This is one of the most important changes.

Right now replication is mostly a free stochastic process. Instead, replication should require conditions like:

* access to a favorable patch
* low local immune pressure
* enough nutrient in that patch
* attachment time or colonization age

New knobs:

* `PATCH_NUTRIENT_CAPACITY`
* `PATCH_REGEN_RATE`
* `REPLICATION_REQUIRES_ATTACHMENT`
* `REPLICATION_REQUIRES_MIN_PATCH_RESOURCE`
* `LOCAL_CARRYING_CAPACITY`
* `IMMUNE_PRESSURE_REPLICATION_PENALTY`

Why this helps:
It kills the exploit where four bacteria just run to four corners and print population. Real *Pseudomonas* needs a niche, resources, and time. It adapts to host metabolites and local conditions; it does not profit equally from every empty square. ([PMC][4])

Good effect on algorithms:
Bacteria must decide where to settle. Macrophages must decide which patches to deny.

---

### 4) Make biofilm a real state transition with entry and exit costs

Do not let quorum instantly turn into a local passive buff.

Use a richer state machine:

* planktonic
* attached
* microcolony
* mature biofilm
* dispersing

New knobs:

* `ATTACHMENT_TIME_REQUIRED`
* `BIOFILM_BUILD_COST`
* `BIOFILM_DISPERSAL_DELAY`
* `C_DI_GMP_LEVEL`
* `BIOFILM_MOTILITY_PENALTY`
* `BIOFILM_REPLICATION_MODIFIER`
* `SURFACE_REQUIRED_FOR_BIOFILM`

Why this helps:
Now the bacteria must decide whether to trade movement for fortress-building. That is biologically grounded: motility and biofilm are opposing modes regulated in part by c-di-GMP. ([PMC][2])

Good effect on algorithms:
This creates timing decisions instead of cheap buffs.

---

### 5) Add neutrophil recruitment as a delayed consequence of macrophage sensing

This is probably the single best biology-driven addition.

Macrophages in alveolar infection are important early sentinels and help drive neutrophil recruitment, while neutrophils are the main acute killers in *P. aeruginosa* lung infection. ([PMC][1])

New knobs:

* `CHEMOKINE_RELEASE_PER_CONTACT`
* `CHEMOKINE_DECAY`
* `NEUTROPHIL_RECRUITMENT_THRESHOLD`
* `NEUTROPHIL_ARRIVAL_DELAY`
* `MAX_NEUTROPHIL_POOL`
* `NEUTROPHIL_LIFESPAN`
* `NEUTROPHIL_COLLATERAL_DAMAGE`

Why this helps:
Now the macrophage is not just a solo killer. It can choose between:

* direct pursuit
* local containment
* signaling for reinforcements

Good effect on algorithms:
This creates a genuinely rich control problem:
“Do I chase now, or hold and recruit?”

---

### 6) Add tissue damage as a first-class score

You asked exactly the right question earlier: can you show the cost of clearance? Yes, and you should.

New knobs:

* `TISSUE_DAMAGE_FROM_NEUTROPHILS`
* `TISSUE_DAMAGE_FROM_BACTERIA`
* `DAMAGE_FROM_PROLONGED_INFLAMMATION`
* `HOST_UTILITY = bacteria_cleared - alpha*tissue_damage - beta*immune_usage`

Why this helps:
It prevents the host from having one dumb objective and gives you more publishable metrics.

Good effect on algorithms:
The host may prefer a low-damage containment strategy over maximal aggression.

## Three strong biology-driven pathways you can take

## Pathway 1: Acute alveolar hotspot model

This is the one I would recommend first.

### Story

A few *P. aeruginosa* land in one or several alveolar patches. Resident macrophages patrol locally. If bacterial burden grows or contact persists, macrophages and epithelium raise chemokines, recruiting neutrophils with delay. If the host contains the hotspot early, damage is low. If delayed, neutrophil influx is larger and tissue damage rises. This is strongly aligned with the known acute lung story. ([PMC][1])

### Why it is algorithmically rich

Bacteria:

* choose attach/disperse
* decide whether to build microcolonies
* exploit local resources
* switch modes under immune pressure

Host:

* patrol
* detect
* contain
* recruit
* manage collateral damage

### Best new knobs

* compartments
* local sensing
* delayed neutrophils
* attachment-dependent replication
* tissue damage

### Why this is a strong first paper

It gives a clean adversarial benchmark with clear biological narrative and measurable trade-offs.

---

## What I would not do first

I would not start by:

* making both sides much more MCTS-heavy
* adding many macrophages immediately
* keeping the 30×30 empty board and just tweaking ratios

That would make compute cost rise while preserving the structural exploit.

## A concrete redesign process

### Step 1: Define the benchmark story first

Pick one story only for version 1.

I recommend:
**acute alveolar hotspot with delayed neutrophil recruitment**

Because it is both biologically grounded and algorithmically rich.

### Step 2: Split the simulator into phases

Instead of one undifferentiated fight, use:

* seeding phase
* local sensing phase
* recruitment phase
* containment / escalation phase

This mirrors the real infection flow better. ([PMC][1])

### Step 3: Replace global replication with patch-based colonization

No more free everywhere replication.

### Step 4: Replace instant quorum buff with attachment state machine

No more instant local defense bonus just because neighbors are near.

### Step 5: Add host utility, not just host victory

Track:

* time to clearance
* tissue damage
* neutrophils recruited
* macrophage survival
* fraction of colonized patches
* whether bacteria reached persistent state

### Step 6: Only then tune counts and algorithms

First fix the world. Then compare agents.

## A balanced starting configuration I would actually build

For the acute alveolar hotspot path:

* 4 to 6 compartments instead of one blank 30×30 board
* 1 resident macrophage per 1–2 compartments
* 2–4 initial bacteria in one or two compartments
* no free global nutrients; use patch resources
* bacteria local sensing radius 3–5
* macrophage sensing radius 5–7
* neutrophil recruitment threshold after repeated local contact
* neutrophil arrival delay 5–15 steps
* replication only if attached or in resource-rich patch
* biofilm needs 2–3 consecutive turns of attachment plus local density
* tissue damage rises with neutrophil crowding and prolonged bacterial persistence

This would already be far richer than your current board.

## My blunt recommendation

Build **Pathway 1** first:

**Acute alveolar hotspot → macrophage detects and contains → chemokine threshold crossed → neutrophils arrive with delay → host tries to clear before tissue damage and bacterial microcolony stabilization escalate.**

Why this is the best choice:

* it fixes your current corner-scatter exploit
* it uses the real biological division of labor between macrophages and neutrophils
* it creates rich sequential decision making
* it does not require massive multi-agent planning on day one
* it gives publishable metrics beyond win rate ([PMC][1])

And the single biggest mental shift is this:

> Stop treating the environment as empty space with roaming agents.
> Start treating it as a set of local infection niches with delayed immune escalation and costly state transitions.

That is the pathway to both balance and richer interaction.
